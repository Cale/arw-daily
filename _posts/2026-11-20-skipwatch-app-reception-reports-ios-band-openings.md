*The following is a message from the creator of [SkipWatch](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skipwatch/id6825989507):*

You ever notice ham radio has a fancy word for everything except "I have no idea"?

Nobody says "I don't know if the band is open." No, no. We say propagation. Propagation! Sounds like something you need a permit for. Sounds like a gardening technique. "Harold's out back, propagating the tomatoes."

And how do we find out about this propagation? Predictions. Forecasts. Models. Solar flux, K index, A index, and a chart with so many colors it looks like a weather map from a planet nobody's ever visited. These are the folks who'll tell you, with total confidence, that 20 meters SHOULD be open to Japan.

Should. "Should" is the word people use right before they let you down. "The check should be in the mail." "This should only take a minute." "The band should be open."

So you believe them. You go out in the backyard. You throw a rope over a tree, miss, hit the gutter, try again, hit the dog. You run fifty feet of coax that cost more per foot than your first car. You fire up the rig. And you hear... nothing. Static. The sound of the universe clearing its throat. You call CQ for forty minutes like a guy in the yard yelling for a dog that ran off in 1997.

### Enter SkipWatch

Now along comes [SkipWatch](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skipwatch/id6825989507). SkipWatch does something radical. Something the guessing business doesn't want you to know about.

It doesn't GUESS. It checks.

Here's the trick. Right now, all over the planet, thousands of stations are running FT8, FT4, WSPR and the other digital modes, and their computers are quietly writing down every signal they hear and reporting it to a site called PSK Reporter. Who heard whom. What band. How loud. From where. It's the biggest gossip network on Earth, and for once in human history, the gossip is USEFUL.

SkipWatch looks at your grid square and asks one simple question: who's hearing people around here, right now? Then it hands you an answer a human being can understand:

"Yes, 20m · Open to Japan."

That's it. Not "conditions appear favorable for mid-latitude paths given moderate solar activity." Just "Yes. Twenty meters. Japan."

Every band from 160 meters to 6 meters gets one of three words: Open, Some paths, or Closed. Ten or more reports? Open. Three to nine? Some paths. Fewer than that? Closed. Go mow the lawn. Three words! Do you know how hard it is to get anybody in this hobby to use three words? I've seen a guy take twelve minutes to give a signal report.

### The tour

**Now.** The big question up top: should I get on the air? Then every band, with real stations behind every answer; tap one and it shows you who's hearing your area and where they are. There's a Sun card with the solar numbers, NOAA space weather alerts, and National Weather Service warnings, including a lightning banner that tells you it's time to disconnect your antennas. Think about that. An app that tells you to unplug before the sky unplugs you. Free of charge. The sky charges a LOT.

**POTA.** Parks on the Air. Grown adults driving to a state park to sit at a picnic table with a radio and a folding antenna, on PURPOSE. I love these people. They're the last honest hobbyists in America. SkipWatch shows who's activating right now, filtered for your rig and your mode, and tells you ""Can I work them?"": Heard, Open, Some paths, or Line of sight. And here's the part that got me: SkipWatch isn't affiliated with POTA, so I wrote and asked permission to use their spot feed. Asked! Nicely!

**Map.** Type any grid or any callsign and you get lines to every station that heard it, colored by band. Look up your own call and see exactly who decoded you last night. Finally, proof somebody out there is listening. Therapy costs two hundred bucks an hour. This is free.

**Point.** A true-north compass with bearing, distance and long path to anywhere you aim it. It tells you which way to run your dipole or end-fed wire, where to point the boom on your beam, and for verticals and mobile whips it says, and I'm quoting here, ""no aiming needed."" The vertical antenna. The golden retriever of antennas. Loves everybody equally. Not too bright. Doesn't care where you point it.

**Greyline.** The newest trick, rolling out in version 1.2. That stretch around sunrise and sunset, when the sun is just below the horizon and the low bands go long. Twilight. The ionosphere's happy hour. SkipWatch does the sun math right on your phone and tells you when your greyline starts, when you and a far-off station share one, and shades night and day right on the Map. A timing hint, mind you, not a promise. It's the only thing in this hobby that admits it's a hint.

**Settings.** Up to six station setups: your radio, your power, your antenna, your bands, your modes. Because 5 watts into an end-fed is a different conversation than 100 watts into a beam, and SkipWatch knows the difference. It even has a red night mode for summits and campouts, so you don't blow out your night vision. Nothing says "serious outdoorsman" like a phone glowing like a photo darkroom.

### What it DOESN'T do

Here's my favorite part. You know how "free" apps work these days. You download it, and it wants your name, your email, your location, your contacts, your blood type, and the name of your first pet. Then it shows you an ad for socks you looked at once in 2019.

SkipWatch has no accounts. No ads. No analytics. No tracking. No server. It doesn't know who you are, and it doesn't WANT to know. It's the healthiest relationship I've got. I hope my wife isn't reading this. 

And the manners on this thing! It only asks PSK Reporter for data once every five minutes. Total. Once! I've got relatives who can't wait five minutes between asking for money.

### Who it's for

The brand new Technician who passed the test and has no clue what to do next. The General with a dipole and a dream. The QRP guy with five watts and a LOT of opinions. The POTA hunter at his kitchen table and the POTA activator at her picnic table. The rotator crowd. The contester who hasn't slept since Thursday. And the guy with the eight-thousand-dollar station who just wants an app to tell him he's right.

Basically, anybody who's ever stood in a backyard holding a coil of wire, staring at the sky, asking it a question.

### Why it matters

Ham radio is the hobby that costs "nothing." Nothing! Except the radio. And the antenna. And the coax. And the connectors. And the tuner. And the OTHER tuner. And the tower. And the conversation with your spouse about the tower.

So the one thing you should never waste is an evening. SkipWatch saves you that evening. Check the band before you climb the tree. If it says Open, go get 'em. If it says Closed, believe it, put the kettle on, and go read QST like a civilized person.

And it's free. Staying free. I could charge a few bucks for it; I won't, because I believe ham radio should be open to ALL. Imagine that. A guy who built something useful and just... gave it to everybody. In this economy.

The sky doesn't lie, folks. It never did. We just finally built something that listens to it.

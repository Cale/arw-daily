*The following is a press release from [RepeaterLive](https://repeaterlive.com/):*

A ham recently re-checked the 100 repeaters programmed in his HT. Forty were dead — and his list was only four years old. Every operator knows the problem: repeater directories go stale, and you find out on a summit, calling into static.

**RepeaterLive** ([repeaterlive.com](https://repeaterlive.com/)), a new live repeater map built by Daniel Régis, VE2DRG, of Québec, attacks the problem by letting the data age honestly. Operators confirm machines from the field with one-tap check-ins and optional 1–5 signal reports. Every listing shows who last heard it and when, reception dots show where each repeater is actually being heard, and a 90-day cycle automatically flags anything nobody can confirm — no listing sits "verified" forever.

The map currently covers **9,500+** repeaters across the US, Canada, the UK, France, and South Korea, with regional offline packs and **free CHIRP export** — one repeater or a whole area, straight into any radio. Access to the data is free and will stay that way.

The project is also working directly with frequency coordination bodies: this week, Oregon became the first state fully aligned to its coordination council's published records, with 238 repeaters added and 52 corrected against ORRC data.

For clubs, a dedicated tier offers CSV bulk import of an entire fleet, up to five shared administrators, an activity dashboard with silence alerts, and a club-verified badge.

"Directories rot — that's not a criticism of anyone, it's physics," says Régis. "The only data source that stays current is the operators using the machines every day. RepeaterLive just gives them a one-tap way to be heard."

Feedback and corrections: every repeater card has a "suggest a correction" link, and the builder answers every email.

[ADRCS](https://adrcs.org/adrcs/) has posted an [extensive update](https://adrcs.org/adrcs/news/) to their ongoing [IP400 project](https://ip400.adrcs.org/). The updates, currently in beat testing, aim to bring mesh networking to any off the shelf radio. A new OFDM data mode will push data rates up to 57 kbit/s.

> The firmware, now known as the Unified Firmware Platform (UFP), adds Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), a modern-day technique that is used in cellular telephony, cable modems and digital subscriber lines. Although in those applications is uses direct on-air modulation, for amateur use it was implemented to operate within the bounds of the audio range of FM transceivers, therefore designated as OFDM-AB. By selecting a bandwidth that is matched to the radio capabilities, coupled with various data constellations, rates from as low as 2600bits/second up to as fast as 57000 bits/second have been realized.

For a deep dive into these developments, see the [most recent issue of Zero Retries](https://www.zeroretries.org/p/zero-retries-0259?open=false#%C2%A7breaking-ofdm-ab-ip400-refactored). Steve Stroh (N8GNJ) provides a detailed introduction to the entirety of the IP400 Project.

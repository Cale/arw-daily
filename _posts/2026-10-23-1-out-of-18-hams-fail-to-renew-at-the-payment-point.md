*By Rob Rosenberger (K4HST)*

The FCC [weekly archives](https://www.fcc.gov/uls/transactions/daily-weekly#weekly-files) include data on call sign renewal payments. Let’s analyze the October 10th archive to discover that 1 out of 18 hams filing for renewal failed in their attempt(s) to pay a $35 fee.

Call sign renewals fall into three major categories: (1) those who successfully renewed, (2) those who failed to pay after applying for renewal, and (3) those who never applied for renewal. A fourth minor category lumps a few renewals that got dismissed for administrative reasons. A snapshot for the last two years including clubs (but excluding the October 2025 gov’t shutdown) reveals on average:

- 3,901 hams per month successfully renewed their call sign. That’s 79.38% of the total.
- 227 hams per month failed the payment process after applying for renewal. That’s 4.63% of the total.
- 783 hams per month failed to apply for renewal. This attrition amounts to 15.93% of the total.

These payment failures exclude initial license grants, and they exclude operators who applied for renewal [after the grace period expired](https://wireless2.fcc.gov/UlsApp/ApplicationSearch/applAdminHistory.jsp?applID=15050218). (They show up in the “failed to apply” category.) Doing the math, we see that almost exactly 1 out of 18 hams who applied for renewal failed in their attempt(s) to pay.

[![ULS attrition](https://assets.midnightcheese.com/amateur-radio-daily/images/uls-attrition-10-26.png)](https://assets.midnightcheese.com/amateur-radio-daily/images/uls-attrition-10-26.png)

Some hams failed multiple times to pay, and KK6UPP holds the top spot with [seven attempts](https://wireless2.fcc.gov/UlsApp/UlsSearch/allApplications.jsp?licKey=3708023) to achieve success. W2ANN failed to make payment with three of his four renewal applications; in one other attempt he probably filed an incomplete application. Luckily, W2ANN has almost a year of grace period left to make a fifth try.

Others were … “not” so lucky. I’ve written the [sad tale of KE6MIT](https://daily.hamweekly.com/2026/09/call-sign-grace-period-a-hard-lesson-learned/) whose call sign expired after multiple attempts to pay. Over the last two years, **431 call signs lapsed** and 1,221 remained in grace period after those operators failed 1.1 times on average to pay a renewal fee.

That “1.1 times on average” reveals a bigger problem: 92% of hams who failed to make a payment the first time simply walked away.

Now, we might ask “did they not [call the FCC help line](https://www.fcc.gov/wtbhelp)?” Maybe yes, maybe no, but it’s a fact that U.S. gov’t payment systems suffer [major disparities](https://www.equinetacademy.com/blog/ux-metrics-how-to-measure-the-success-of-a-design/) between the user **experience** and the user **interface**. Ask any military member who applied to DoD for any type of refund!

The FCC’s license renewal journey crosses two portals with two sets of login credentials — and more than nine out of ten folks who [stumbled into the labyrinth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ckUCj8-jbg) decided not to map it out a second time. I saw these payment difficulties firsthand when KC9GSI and KI4RED asked for my help to retrieve their call signs from grace period.

In one sentence: “We need to focus on operators who fail the payment process.”

First things first. We need to **discover** people before they lose their call sign. That’s where I come in. I’m the driving force behind a series of free PDF call books, and you can download one listing [operators within three months of losing their call sign](https://w4zbb.org/resources/) sorted by city & state.

Second, ham radio clubs should **contact** local operators who may need help with renewal. Download the free PDF listed above to find vulnerable hams in nearby towns. Clubs can reduce the “1 out of 18” problem at the local level. I expect [our club](https://w4zbb.org/resources/)’s letter to these folks will start off with “Do you need help getting your call sign back? We missed you when Hurricane Isaias slammed our community!”

Third, ARRL should **work** with the FCC to reduce this problem at the national level. Can the FCC [improve the payment labyrinth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_RKGLjdaAlo)? Should they [advise folks to check & update their email address](https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/support/knowledge-base/common-amateur-filing-tasks/common-amateur-filing-task-renewing) prior to filing a renewal application? Does ARRL reach out to their (in)active membership when a call sign approaches the end of its grace period?

Now that we recognize the “1 out of 18” problem, we can seek to know why it happens and we can find other ways to tackle it. For example, I’m going to print this and mail it to my congressman. Let’s get QRL!
